# Vocal Registers — Reference Library

This file is part of the Music Director `library/`. It provides **reference knowledge** about vocal registers. It does not contain orchestration rules; those remain in `instructions/performance.md`.

Consult via `library/index.md`.

---

### Chest voice

- **Term:** Chest voice
- **Aliases:** chest register, modal voice (low/mid), heavy register
- **Category:** vocal-registers
- **Definition:** Register produced with full vocal fold closure and thicker fold mass, yielding a rich, powerful tone in the lower/mid range.
- **Audible effect:** Warm, grounded, powerful, speech-like low range.
- **When to use:** Low verses, powerful phrases, grounding.
- **When to avoid / limitations:** Very high pitches (strain), light/airy effects.
- **Compatible combinations:** Belting, twang, legato.
- **Conflicts:** With falsetto/whistle; forcing chest high causes strain.
- **Knowledge type:** consolidated
- **Source:** Sundberg, J. (1987). *The Science of the Singing Voice*; Miller, R. (1996). *The Structure of Singing*.

### Head voice

- **Term:** Head voice
- **Aliases:** head register, light register
- **Category:** vocal-registers
- **Definition:** Register with thinner vocal fold mass and higher laryngeal position, producing a lighter, resonant tone in the upper range. Terminology varies by approach (see Falsetto): some pedagogies treat head voice and falsetto as distinct; others use "falsetto" broadly for light high singing. Here, head voice denotes the more resonant, connected light register.
- **Audible effect:** Lighter, ringing, resonant high notes without breathiness.
- **When to use:** High melodies, classical/pop high phrases, clarity in upper range.
- **When to avoid / limitations:** When power/heaviness is needed (use chest/mix).
- **Compatible combinations:** Vibrato, legato, vowel modification.
- **Conflicts:** With chest (abrupt break) if not blended.
- **Knowledge type:** consolidated
- **Source:** Sundberg, J. (1987). *The Science of the Singing Voice*.

### Falsetto

- **Term:** Falsetto
- **Aliases:** false voice, loft register
- **Category:** vocal-registers
- **Definition:** Register produced with elongated, thin vocal folds and incomplete closure, yielding a breathy, flute-like tone above modal range. Terminology varies by approach: in some pedagogies (e.g., classical), "head voice" and "falsetto" are treated as distinct registers; in others (e.g., contemporary/commercial), "falsetto" is used loosely for any light high register. The distinction here (head voice = resonant/lighter with more closure; falsetto = breathier, flutier, less closure) reflects a common contemporary usage, not a universal standard.
- **Audible effect:** Airy, ethereal, light, often breathy.
- **When to use:** High, soft, intimate or ethereal moments.
- **When to avoid / limitations:** Powerful high notes (use mix/belt), when full tone is needed.
- **Compatible combinations:** Breathily singing, reverb, legato.
- **Conflicts:** With belting; can sound weak if overused.
- **Knowledge type:** consolidated
- **Source:** Sundberg, J. (1987). *The Science of the Singing Voice*; Miller, R. (1996). *The Structure of Singing*.

### Mixed voice

- **Term:** Mixed voice
- **Aliases:** mix, blended register, middle voice
- **Category:** vocal-registers
- **Definition:** Coordination blending chest and head register characteristics to navigate the passaggio smoothly.
- **Audible effect:** Balanced, connected range without obvious break.
- **When to use:** Transitions, sustained high phrases, contemporary pop/R&B.
- **When to avoid / limitations:** When pure chest or pure head color is desired.
- **Compatible combinations:** Belting, vowel modification, vibrato.
- **Conflicts:** Requires training; poor mix can sound strained.
- **Knowledge type:** consolidated
- **Source:** Estill, J. (2005). *Primer of Compulsory Figures for Voice*; Miller, R. (1996). *The Structure of Singing*.

### Vocal fry

- **Term:** Vocal fry
- **Aliases:** pulse register, creaky voice, glottal fry
- **Category:** vocal-registers
- **Definition:** Lowest register, produced with very short, irregular vocal fold pulses and low airflow. It is a phonation mode (pulse register), not a register equivalent to chest voice or falsetto.
- **Audible effect:** Creaky, rattling, low, rough low-end texture.
- **When to use:** Stylistic low endings, intimate/edgy texture, fry at phrase ends.
- **When to avoid / limitations:** Sustained singing, when clarity/pitch is needed; overuse can be unhealthy.
- **Compatible combinations:** Conversational delivery, low chest endings.
- **Conflicts:** With sustained melodic singing; not a full register for melody.
- **Knowledge type:** consolidated
- **Source:** Sundberg, J. (1987). *The Science of the Singing Voice*; Titze, I. (1994). *Principles of Voice Production*. Prentice Hall.

### Whistle register

- **Term:** Whistle register
- **Aliases:** flute register, whistle tone
- **Category:** vocal-registers
- **Definition:** Highest register, produced with very thin, tightly stretched folds; extremely high pitches.
- **Audible effect:** Piercing, flute-like, very high.
- **When to use:** Special effects, climactic high notes (pop/R&B).
- **When to avoid / limitations:** Most genres; requires specialized technique.
- **Compatible combinations:** Falsetto (transition), reverb.
- **Conflicts:** With chest/belt; not for sustained melody in most styles.
- **Knowledge type:** consolidated
- **Source:** Sundberg, J. (1987). *The Science of the Singing Voice*; Miller, R. (1996). *The Structure of Singing*.
