# Vocal Delivery — Reference Library

This file is part of the Music Director `library/`. It provides **reference knowledge** about vocal delivery modes. It does not contain orchestration rules; those remain in `instructions/performance.md`.

Consult via `library/index.md`.

---

### Whispered

- **Term:** Whispered
- **Aliases:** whisper, breathy whisper
- **Category:** vocal-delivery
- **Definition:** Phonation with minimal vocal fold closure, producing a breathy, non-pitched or lightly pitched sound. Distinct from breathy singing (breathy voice): whisper is essentially unvoiced, with little or no periodic vocal-fold vibration, whereas breathy singing is voiced phonation with incomplete closure and audible air. In practice, "whispered" and "breathy" can overlap at the boundary, but they are not identical.
- **Audible Effect:** Intimate, airy, close-mic feel.
- **When to use:** Intimate verses, suspense, soft openings, ASMR-like textures.
- **When to avoid:** Loud mixes, when projection is needed, high-energy sections.
- **Compatible combinations:** Breathily singing, sparse arrangement, rubato.
- **Conflicts or limitations:** With belting/twang; can be inaudible in dense mixes.
- **Example of Suno-ready direction:** Whispered
- **Source:** Sundberg, J. (1987). *The Science of the Singing Voice*; McKinney, J. (1994). *The Diagnosis and Correction of Vocal Faults*.

### Spoken word

- **Term:** Spoken word
- **Aliases:** speech-like, recitative, parlando
- **Category:** vocal-delivery
- **Definition:** Delivery that follows speech rhythm/pitch rather than sung melody; may include slight pitch variation.
- **Audible Effect:** Narrative, conversational, dramatic.
- **When to use:** Verses, storytelling, bridges, dramatic monologue.
- **When to avoid:** Melodic hooks, when singing is expected throughout.
- **Compatible combinations:** Rap, rubato, sparse accompaniment.
- **Conflicts or limitations:** With sustained singing; can reduce musicality if overused.
- **Example of Suno-ready direction:** Spoken word
- **Source:** Miller, R. (1996). *The Structure of Singing*. Schirmer.

### Rap delivery

- **Term:** Rap delivery
- **Aliases:** rapping, rhythmic speech, flow
- **Category:** vocal-delivery
- **Definition:** Rhythmic, rhymed speech delivered with precise timing; pitch may be monotone or slightly varied.
- **Audible Effect:** Percussive, rhythmic, groove-locked.
- **When to use:** Rap verses, hip-hop, spoken hooks, rhythmic storytelling.
- **When to avoid:** Melodic choruses, ballads, when sustained tone is needed.
- **Compatible combinations:** Monotone, twang, rhythmic phrasing.
- **Conflicts or limitations:** With legato singing; can clash with slow tempos.
- **Example of Suno-ready direction:** Rap delivery
- **Source:** Adams, K. (2009). On the Metrical Techniques of Flow in Rap Music. *Music Theory Online*, 15(5).

### Conversational

- **Term:** Conversational
- **Aliases:** talk-sing, speech-like singing, intimate talk
- **Category:** vocal-delivery
- **Definition:** Singing that mimics natural speech inflection and rhythm, with relaxed pitch and phrasing.
- **Audible Effect:** Intimate, natural, unforced.
- **When to use:** Verses, indie/folk, singer-songwriter, intimate passages.
- **When to avoid:** Anthemic choruses, when power/projection is needed.
- **Compatible combinations:** Breathily singing, rubato, sparse arrangement.
- **Conflicts or limitations:** With belting/twang; can lack projection.
- **Example of Suno-ready direction:** Conversational
- **Source:** Miller, R. (1996). *The Structure of Singing*; Sundberg, J. (1987). *The Science of the Singing Voice*.

### Belting

- **Term:** Belting
- **Aliases:** belt, belted voice, chest-dominant high singing
- **Category:** vocal-delivery
- **Definition:** Powerful, speech-like singing in the upper range using chest-dominant coordination with twang; high volume and clarity.
- **Audible Effect:** Strong, bright, cutting, energetic.
- **When to use:** Choruses, anthems, climaxes, pop/rock/musical theatre.
- **When to avoid:** Soft/intimate passages, classical contexts, when vocal health is a concern (use with technique).
- **Compatible combinations:** Twang, vowel modification, chest voice.
- **Conflicts or limitations:** With breathy/whispered delivery; can strain if untrained.
- **Example of Suno-ready direction:** Belting
- **Source:** Estill, J. (2005). *Primer of Compulsory Figures for Voice*; Sundberg, J. (1987). *The Science of the Singing Voice*.
