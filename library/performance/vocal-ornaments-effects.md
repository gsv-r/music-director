# Vocal Ornaments and Effects — Reference Library

This file is part of the Music Director `library/`. It provides **reference knowledge** about vocal ornaments and effects. It does not contain orchestration rules; those remain in `instructions/performance.md`.

Consult via `library/index.md`.

---

### Vibrato

- **Term:** Vibrato
- **Aliases:** vocal vibrato, natural vibrato
- **Category:** vocal-ornaments-effects
- **Definition:** Periodic, slight oscillation of pitch (and sometimes amplitude) during a sustained note.
- **Audible Effect:** Warmth, richness, expressiveness, "live" quality.
- **When to use:** Sustained notes, emotional peaks, classical/pop ballads.
- **When to avoid:** Straight-tone passages, modern pop (sometimes), when purity is desired.
- **Compatible combinations:** Messa di voce, legato, head voice.
- **Conflicts or limitations:** With straight tone; excessive vibrato sounds uncontrolled.
- **Example of Suno-ready direction:** Vibrato
- **Source:** Sundberg, J. (1987). *The Science of the Singing Voice*; Miller, R. (1996). *The Structure of Singing*.

### Runs

- **Term:** Runs
- **Aliases:** vocal runs, riffs, melodic runs
- **Category:** vocal-ornaments-effects
- **Definition:** Rapid sequence of notes (often scale/arpeggio-based) sung on a single syllable or across syllables. Relationship to melisma: a run is typically a rapid melismatic passage; melisma is the broader term for multiple notes on one syllable. In practice the terms overlap, but "run" emphasizes speed/agility, while "melisma" emphasizes the many-notes-per-syllable structure.
- **Audible Effect:** Agile, ornamental, flashy, soulful.
- **When to use:** R&B, gospel, pop, climaxes, ad-libs.
- **When to avoid:** Dense lyrics, fast tempos, when clarity is needed.
- **Compatible combinations:** Melisma, vibrato, belting.
- **Conflicts or limitations:** With legato clarity; can obscure lyrics.
- **Example of Suno-ready direction:** Runs
- **Source:** Miller, R. (1996). *The Structure of Singing*.

### Melisma

- **Term:** Melisma
- **Aliases:** melismatic singing, multiple notes per syllable
- **Category:** vocal-ornaments-effects
- **Definition:** Singing multiple notes on a single syllable. Relationship to runs: melisma is the general term for multiple notes per syllable; a run is a rapid melismatic passage. The terms overlap in practice; "melisma" stresses the structure, "run" stresses speed and agility.
- **Audible Effect:** Ornate, expressive, flowing; extends syllable.
- **When to use:** Emotional peaks, gospel/R&B, classical, emphasis.
- **When to avoid:** When lyric clarity/speed is needed, dense verses.
- **Compatible combinations:** Runs, vibrato, legato.
- **Conflicts or limitations:** With syllabic/staccato delivery; can reduce intelligibility.
- **Example of Suno-ready direction:** Melisma
- **Source:** Sundberg, J. (1987). *The Science of the Singing Voice*; Miller, R. (1996). *The Structure of Singing*.

### Pitch bending

- **Term:** Pitch bending
- **Aliases:** scoop, slide, bend
- **Category:** vocal-ornaments-effects
- **Definition:** Continuous glide between pitches, often at phrase starts/ends or for expressive effect.
- **Audible Effect:** Expressive, bluesy, human, sometimes "scooping".
- **When to use:** Blues, pop, R&B, emotional transitions.
- **When to avoid:** When precise pitch/intonation is required (classical).
- **Compatible combinations:** Runs, vibrato, legato.
- **Conflicts or limitations:** With strict intonation; can sound sloppy if overused. Portamento (a glide between notes) is related but distinct; it is listed separately from aliases here.
- **Example of Suno-ready direction:** Pitch bending
- **Source:** Sundberg, J. (1987). *The Science of the Singing Voice*.

### Growl

- **Term:** Growl
- **Aliases:** grit, rasp, vocal distortion, fry scream (related)
- **Category:** vocal-ornaments-effects
- **Definition:** Rough, distorted tone produced by controlled vocal fold collision/ventricular fold involvement or fry; adds grit.
- **Audible Effect:** Aggressive, raspy, edgy, rock/metal texture.
- **When to use:** Rock, metal, aggressive choruses, emphasis.
- **When to avoid:** Soft/intimate, classical, when vocal health is a concern; untrained use risks damage.
- **Compatible combinations:** Twang, belting, chest voice.
- **Conflicts or limitations:** With breathy/clean tone; can mask melody.
- **Example of Suno-ready direction:** Growl
- **Source:** Estill, J. (2005). *Primer of Compulsory Figures for Voice*; Sundberg, J. (1987). *The Science of the Singing Voice*.

### Cry/sob

- **Term:** Cry/sob
- **Aliases:** sob quality, cry tone, emotional cry
- **Category:** vocal-ornaments-effects
- **Definition:** Vocal quality with compressed, emotional tone and often a "catch" or sob in the voice, associated with sadness/yearning.
- **Audible Effect:** Emotional, vulnerable, tearful quality.
- **When to use:** Sad ballads, emotional peaks, country/pop.
- **When to avoid:** Upbeat/energetic, when neutrality is needed.
- **Compatible combinations:** Breathily singing, vibrato, rubato.
- **Conflicts or limitations:** With bright/twangy delivery; can be overdone.
- **Example of Suno-ready direction:** Cry/sob
- **Source:** Miller, R. (1996). *The Structure of Singing*; Estill, J. (2005). *Primer of Compulsory Figures for Voice*.
