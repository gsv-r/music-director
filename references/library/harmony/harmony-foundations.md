# Harmony and tonality reference

Local vocabulary for tonal identity and color. Style owns the global choice;
Arrangement owns harmonic events that change form. Consult via `index.md`.
Western tonal terms are not universal: do not impose them on traditions that
organize pitch differently.

### Tonal center and key

The pitch a passage gravitates toward, with or without a named key. Sounds like
a home or its deliberate absence. Use when it defines the identity. Ambiguity
is a valid choice. Sources: Lerdahl and Jackendoff (1983); Krumhansl (1990).

### Scales and modes

The pitch collection and its characteristic color. Sounds major, minor, modal
or otherwise inflected. Use when that color shapes the piece. Do not reduce a
tradition to one scale.

### Consonance and dissonance

The relative stability of intervals and chords, including chord color. Sounds
resolved or tense. Use to support the emotion of a line. Treat it as a practice
that varies by style, not a universal law. Sources: Meyer (1956); Tymoczko (2011).

### Harmonic function and cadence

The tendency of chords to move toward resolution. Sounds like departure and
arrival. Use for phrases and formal arrivals. Non-functional harmony is equally
valid when the piece calls for it. Source: Aldwell, Schachter and Cadwallader (2018).

### Harmonic rhythm

The rate at which chords change. Sounds busy when fast, static when slow. Use
when the rate affects momentum or a section's balance. Do not impose a default
rate. Source: Lerdahl and Jackendoff (1983).
