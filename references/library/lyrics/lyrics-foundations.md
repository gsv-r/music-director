# Lyrics reference

Local vocabulary for meaning in sung text. Lyric rules, prosody, singability,
titles and hooks live in `references/instructions/lyrics.md`. Consult via
`index.md`. These are possibilities, not requirements.

### Layered meaning

Meaning beyond the literal statement, grounded in the words and the context.
Sounds like subtext when a line carries more than it says. Use when the brief
calls for depth. Direct and simple writing is equally valid. Sources: Negus and
Astor (2015); Zyga (2020).

### Imagery and coherence

Concrete images and their development across a song. Sounds grounded when
images recur or change with the sections. Use to give a song observable detail.
Avoid decorative images that do not affect the meaning.

### Song architecture and repetition

The way a returning line or section changes meaning because of its position.
Sounds familiar yet different when context shifts. Use when repetition should
carry development. Exact repetition is also valid. Source: Negus and Astor (2015).

### Lyrics as musical material

Syllabic density, stress, rhyme, assonance and articulation as things that are
heard. Sounds natural when words fit the melody and breathable phrases. Use to
check singability. Do not reduce the words to sound alone. Sources: Pattison,
*Writing Better Lyrics* (2010); Parada-Cabaleiro et al. (2024) on simplicity and
repetition as valid trends, not defects.

### Lexical precision

Choosing the specific word rather than the generic one. Sounds clearer and
more particular. Use when a line could be more exact. Do not make diction
ornate for its own sake; simplicity can carry strong meaning.
