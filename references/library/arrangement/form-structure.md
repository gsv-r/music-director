# Form and structure reference

Local vocabulary for formal events. Arrangement rules live in
`references/instructions/arrangement.md`. Consult via `index.md`.

### Transition as formal event

A passage that changes the perceived form, balance or direction of the piece.
Sounds like a new section or a turn. Use when the change is audible and
structural; a simple fill is not a formal event.

### Climax preparation

A passage that builds toward a peak. Sounds like growing expectation. Use when
the piece needs a prepared high point. Avoid forcing a peak into a song whose
intent is stable.

### Climax release

The arrival and resolution after a build. Sounds like release or payoff. Use
where the tension should resolve. The resolution may be delayed or withheld
when the intent calls for it.

### Formal contrast

A clear difference between sections in density, register, texture or energy.
Sounds like differentiation. Use to keep a returning form from feeling flat.
Contrast is not required; deliberate similarity is valid.

### Repetition with formal variation

A returning section that keeps its identity while one element changes. Sounds
familiar but different. Use when repetition should carry development. Write any
changed lyric in the lyrics field.

### Non-conventional forms

Forms that are not verse-chorus based, such as through-composed, episodic,
circular or accumulative shapes. Use when the material and the brief call for
them. Do not treat the standard song form as the default.
