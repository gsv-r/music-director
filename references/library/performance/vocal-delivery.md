# Vocal delivery reference

Local vocabulary for the basic way a voice is used. Rules and tag limits live
in `references/instructions/performance.md`. Consult via `index.md`.

### Whispered

Mostly unvoiced, breath-driven phonation. Sounds intimate, airy and close.
Use for soft openings, suspense and intimate verses. Avoid in loud mixes or
where projection is needed; it conflicts with belting and dense arrangements.
Source: Sundberg, *The Science of the Singing Voice* (1987).

### Breathy singing

Voiced phonation with incomplete closure, so air remains audible. Softer than
belt, more pitched than whisper. Use for intimate and vulnerable passages.
Avoid when clarity or power is needed. Works with sparse arrangement and rubato.

### Spoken word

Speech rhythm and pitch instead of a sung melody. Sounds narrative and
conversational. Use for verses, storytelling and dramatic monologue. Avoid for
melodic hooks. Source: Miller, *The Structure of Singing* (1996).

### Rap delivery

Rhythmic, rhymed speech with precise timing and little melodic contour. Sounds
percussive and groove-locked. Use in hip-hop, rhythmic hooks and storytelling.
Avoid where sustained tone is needed. Source: Adams (2009), *Music Theory Online*.

### Conversational

Singing that follows natural speech inflection and relaxed phrasing. Sounds
unforced and close. Use in folk, indie and singer-songwriter verses. Avoid in
anthemic choruses where projection is needed.

### Belting

Powerful, speech-like singing in the upper range with chest-dominant
coordination and twang. Sounds bright, strong and energetic. Use in choruses,
anthems and climaxes. Avoid in soft or intimate passages; it conflicts with
whispered delivery and needs healthy technique. Source: Estill; Sundberg (1987).
