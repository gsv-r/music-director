# Vocal Articulation — Reference Library

This file is part of the Music Director `references/library/`. It provides **reference knowledge** about vocal articulation techniques. It does not contain orchestration rules; those remain in `references/instructions/performance.md`.

Consult via `references/library/index.md`: locate the term/alias/intent, then read only this file (or the relevant section).

---

### Legato

- **Term:** Legato
- **Aliases:** smooth connection, connected phrasing
- **Category:** vocal-articulation
- **Definition:** Singing in which notes and syllables are connected without audible breaks; consonants are softened and vowels flow into one another.
- **Audible effect:** Seamless, flowing line; no gaps between notes; syllables merge.
- **When to use:** Lyrical passages, ballads, classical/legato phrases, when continuity and warmth are desired.
- **When to avoid / limitations:** Percussive styles, rap, staccato effects, when clarity of each syllable is needed.
- **Compatible combinations:** Vibrato, messa di voce, breathy singing.
- **Conflicts:** Excessive legato can blur diction; conflicts with staccato/rhythmic precision. Portamento (a glide between notes) is related but distinct; see pitch bending.
- **Knowledge type:** consolidated
- **Source:** Miller, R. (1996). *The Structure of Singing*. Schirmer.

### Staccato

- **Term:** Staccato
- **Aliases:** detached, short notes, marcato (related)
- **Category:** vocal-articulation
- **Definition:** Notes/syllables sung short and detached, with clear separation between them.
- **Audible effect:** Crisp, punctuated, rhythmic delivery.
- **When to use:** Rhythmic hooks, pop choruses, percussive phrasing, clarity.
- **When to avoid / limitations:** Lyrical/legato contexts, when smoothness is desired.
- **Compatible combinations:** Twang, belting, rhythmic phrasing.
- **Conflicts:** With legato; can sound choppy if overused.
- **Knowledge type:** consolidated
- **Source:** Miller, R. (1996). *The Structure of Singing*. Schirmer; Sundberg, J. (1987). *The Science of the Singing Voice*. Northern Illinois University Press.

### Glottal stop

- **Term:** Glottal stop
- **Aliases:** hard glottal attack, glottal onset, coup de glotte
- **Category:** vocal-articulation
- **Definition:** Onset produced by closing the vocal folds before airflow, releasing with a slight "catch".
- **Audible effect:** Hard, percussive attack at start of vowel; edgy.
- **When to use:** Emphasis, dramatic attacks, certain pop/rock styles.
- **When to avoid / limitations:** Classical/legato singing, fragile passages, when breathiness is desired; overuse can cause strain.
- **Compatible combinations:** Belting, twang, grit (with care).
- **Conflicts:** With breathy onset; can be harsh.
- **Knowledge type:** consolidated
- **Source:** Sundberg, J. (1987). *The Science of the Singing Voice*; McKinney, J. (1994). *The Diagnosis and Correction of Vocal Faults*. Genovex.

### Twang

- **Term:** Twang
- **Aliases:** twangy quality, bright twang, belt twang
- **Category:** vocal-articulation
- **Definition:** Bright, piercing quality produced by narrowing the epilaryngeal tube (aryepiglottic sphincter), increasing high-frequency energy.
- **Audible effect:** Cutting, bright, "brassy" edge; projects over accompaniment.
- **When to use:** Belting, country, rock, when projection/clarity is needed.
- **When to avoid / limitations:** Intimate/soft passages, classical legato, when warmth is desired.
- **Compatible combinations:** Belting, grit, chest voice.
- **Conflicts:** With breathy/soft delivery; can sound harsh if overused.
- **Knowledge type:** consolidated
- **Source:** Estill, J. (2005). *Primer of Compulsory Figures for Voice*. Estill Voice Training Systems.

### Vowel modification

- **Term:** Vowel modification
- **Aliases:** vowel tuning, formant tuning, vowel shaping
- **Category:** vocal-articulation
- **Definition:** Adjusting vowel shape (e.g., Ah→Oh→Oo) as pitch ascends to reduce tension and maintain resonance.
- **Audible effect:** Smoother high notes, less strain, more consistent tone.
- **When to use:** Ascending phrases, high notes, belting passages.
- **When to avoid / limitations:** When specific vowel clarity is required (lyric intelligibility), low range.
- **Compatible combinations:** Belting, mixed voice, legato.
- **Conflicts:** Can reduce lyric clarity if overdone.
- **Knowledge type:** consolidated
- **Source:** Miller, R. (1996). *The Structure of Singing*; Sundberg, J. (1987). *The Science of the Singing Voice*.
