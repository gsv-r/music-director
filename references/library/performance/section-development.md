# Section development reference

Local vocabulary for what changes across sections. Arrangement owns formal
structure; these entries describe the audible execution of a passage. Rules
live in `references/instructions/performance.md`. Consult via `index.md`.

### Slow build

Gradual increase of vocal intensity across a section. Sounds like a rise toward
something. Use when a passage moves toward a peak. Unverified as a universal
term; conventional production practice.

### Rising tension

Increasing intensity or urgency without a full release. Sounds like
anticipation. Use before a chorus or a drop. Avoid when stability is intended.

### Big chorus

Open, full delivery on the chorus. Sounds wide and released. Use when the
chorus is the song's center. Avoid defaulting to it; a restrained chorus is valid.

### Final surge

A last lift at the end of the song. Sounds conclusive and heightened. Use when
the ending calls for it. Do not impose it by default; intentional stability is
equally valid.

### Afterglow

Calm energy after a peak. Sounds like a cool-down. Use after a climax or at an
ending that settles. Avoid when the song needs a dry cut.

### Chanted hook

Repeated short vocal phrase on the hook. Sounds communal and memorable. Use in
genres that support chant and call-and-response. Avoid in dense storytelling.

### Breakdown

A stripped passage with most layers removed. Sounds exposed and quieter. Use
for contrast before a return. The layer removal itself is an arrangement
decision, not a performance one.

### Dry stop

Abrupt silence with no tail. Sounds like a hard cut. Use for a dramatic pause
or ending. Avoid when a natural decay serves the piece better.
