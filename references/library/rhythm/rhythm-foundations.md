# Rhythm and groove reference

Local vocabulary for meter, feel and density. Rules live in the Style and
Arrangement modules. Consult via `index.md`.

### Swing and straight feel

Uneven long-short subdivision versus even subdivision. Sounds lilting or
locked. Use the feel the genre calls for; do not assume a fixed swing ratio or
treat straight feel as an absence of groove. Sources: Friberg and Sundström
(2002); Butterfield (2006, 2011); Benadon (2006).

### Shuffle

A triplet-derived bounce with a loping feel, historically variable across
blues, rock, jazz and funk. Use when the genre calls for it. Do not treat it as
a synonym for swing or as one fixed pattern.

### Backbeat

Accent on beats 2 and 4 in 4/4 or equivalent offbeat positions. Sounds
propulsive and is central to many popular styles. Avoid presenting it as
universal; it does not fit every meter or tradition.

### Syncopation

Accent moved to a weak beat or offbeat. Sounds like forward lean and surprise.
Use for rhythmic tension. Avoid overuse that erodes the sense of pulse.
Source: Cooper and Meyer, *The Rhythmic Structure of Music* (1960).

### Groove

The felt interaction of rhythmic layers, timing and listener response. Sounds
like the parts lock together. Use when shaping the global rhythmic identity.
It is multidimensional; do not reduce it to microtiming alone. Sources: Keil
(1987); Iyer (2002); Duman et al. (2024).

### Rhythmic density

Frequency of note onsets over time. High density sounds busy; low density
sounds open. Use when the amount of rhythmic activity changes a section.
Specify what is being counted; do not confuse it with the number of layers or
with perceived energy. Sources: Cooper and Meyer (1960); Senn et al. (2017).
