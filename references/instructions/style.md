# Guidelines for sonic identity

The output of this module is textual: one concise Style description. It gives
the global sound and global movement. It does not decide lyric content, section
marks, layers or local density. It never produces audio. See
`references/instructions/best-practices.md` for the criteria that matter and
`references/instructions/orchestration.md` for universal rules.

## What to write

Order, from most to least important:

1. Genre or subgenre, with an era or scene only when it changes the sound.
2. Tempo: BPM or a tempo feel, with groove and drum pattern when the genre
   calls for it (for example four-on-the-floor, halftime, shuffle, backbeat).
3. Two or three main instruments, each with a function: pulse carrier, groove
   anchor, harmonic bed, counterline, hook, impact layer or texture glue.
4. Voice: timbre (warm, bright, dark, airy, breathy, raspy, clear) plus way of
   singing (intimate, conversational, whispered, restrained, powerful,
   melodic, laid-back) and a technique only when it is heard.
5. Audible production texture, when it shapes the result: dry close vocal,
   tape saturation, spring reverb, room bleed, wide pads.
6. Vocal diction and language, for example `Brazilian Portuguese vocals`.
7. Global movement, when it changes the result: progressive, stable, circular,
   a build into a drop, a late entry. Section-by-section form belongs in the
   lyrics markers, not here.

Fusion: one anchor plus modifiers, at most two or three styles. Do not mix
contradictory genres or add genres and instruments the user did not ask for.

## Guardrails

- Prefer specific, audible descriptors to generic adjectives. "Epic",
  "beautiful", "polished" and "cinematic" need concrete support or must go.
- No technical parameters: no percentages, dB, ms, Hz, structured data or
  tool-specific tags.
- Do not use artist, band, singer or song names as a substitute for description.
- Do not presume voice gender, timbre or intensity when the brief is silent.
- Choose a plausible BPM and key for the genre when the user does not specify
  them, rather than omitting them.
- Contradictory descriptors, functionless instrument lists and decorative
  technical terms are errors. Elements that clash with the proposal need
  explicit intent.
- Exclusions are optional: two or three at most, at the end of the line.
- Four to seven specific descriptors is a heuristic, not a target.

## Language and coherence

The song's language follows the brief, never the language of these documents.
Reflect it in the vocal diction descriptor. The style must fit the emotion of
the lyrics and the tempo the lyrics are written for. Shared decisions are made
once and reused. An intentional contrast requested by the user is preserved.

## References

Translate a provided reference into generic descriptors with the rules in
`references/instructions/orchestration.md`, "Musical references". If it cannot
be accessed, say so in one sentence and ask for the missing data.

## Library consultation

Targeted, only when the decision is material:

- `references/library/rhythm/` for groove, swing or straight feel, shuffle,
  backbeat, syncopation and rhythmic density.
- `references/library/production/` for ambience, space, saturation, reverb,
  delay and compression as audible texture.
- `references/library/harmony/` for tonal center, mode, chord color and
  harmonic rhythm when they define the identity.

Write the decision itself in the style line, not the name of the consulted
entry. Do not repeat per-section choices here.

## Output

Present one concise description. Presentation inside the final delivery
format follows `references/instructions/orchestration.md`, "Final delivery
format (mandatory)".
