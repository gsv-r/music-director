# Guidelines for arrangement (internal plan)

Arrangement owns form and architecture: sections, their order and relative
duration, introductions, interludes and endings, which layers are active, when
they enter and leave, density, texture and transitions. It produces no separate
field: its decisions are written as musical structure in the Style description
and as section markers in the lyrics. It never produces audio.

## Inputs and boundaries

Style supplies the global sound, palette and movement; Lyrics supplies the sung
structure when the piece is vocal. Arrangement consumes both and does not
redefine genre, tempo, voice or lyric content. For an instrumental piece it may
propose a section structure suited to the brief; no scene, narrator or point of
view is required. Ask only when an indispensable structural decision cannot be
inferred.

## Decisions

- Name and order sections from the intent of this composition. No default
  order, no default density for a section type. Instrumental-only sections are
  valid and need no lyric content.
- Give each layer a function. Entries and exits need a musical reason; do not
  add or remove layers mechanically. A breakdown withdraws layers.
- Express relative duration as role or proportion, not as a mandatory timing.
  A transition is a formal event only when it changes the perceived form,
  balance or trajectory.
- Plan internally what changes across a returning section: layer count,
  register, rhythmic density, space, texture, harmonic color. Variation needs a
  reason; exact repetition and stability are valid.
- Growth by adding layers belongs here. Growth by intensity of execution
  belongs to Performance. When both happen, keep them distinguishable.
- Counterlines and instrumental responses must not obscure the lead or the
  sung text. Silence is equally valid.

## Coherence

The plan must fit the Style and the sung structure. Changing the order of sung
sections requires coordination with Lyrics; Arrangement does not rewrite the
text. Performance consumes the plan and must not invent layer entries. If a
discrepancy remains after one pass, report it in the analysis rather than
rewriting repeatedly.

## Library consultation

Use `references/library/arrangement/` when formal decisions are material,
`references/library/rhythm/` for density, meter or syncopation,
`references/library/production/` for texture mechanisms and
`references/library/harmony/` for harmonic events that change the form. Read
only the pointed section. Library knowledge never overrides this module.
