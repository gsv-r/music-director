# Guidelines for lyric composition

The result is textual: structured lyrics with section marks and any audible
indications written into the composition. It never produces audio. See
`references/instructions/best-practices.md` for the criteria that matter and
`references/instructions/orchestration.md` for universal rules.

## Language

Write in the language of the brief unless the user explicitly asks for another.
Preserve that language's natural phrasing, stress, syllabic rhythm and cultural
nuance. If the brief mixes languages and none is clear, ask once. Never default
to English because these documents are in English.

## Structure and density

Duration is a decision from the brief. When none is given, an initial reference
is 40 seconds and 10 sung lines in three blocks; adapt freely. For a longer
piece, a possible reference is verse, verse, pre-chorus, chorus, verse, chorus,
bridge, outro. A rough density reference: 30 to 60 s, 8 to 16 lines; 1 to 2 min,
16 to 28 lines; 2 to 3 min, 28 to 48 lines. These are starting points, not
templates. Exact repetition and stability are valid artistic decisions.

In an instrumental piece no sung text is invented; this field carries the
section structure and instrumental execution indications instead.

## Before writing

Identify the function of the voice from the brief: lead, chant, texture,
narrator or another role. Ask for a concrete scene or point of view only when
the chosen writing depends on them and the brief does not supply them. For
abstract, chant, hypnotic or texture-based writing, ask instead for the
function of the voice and the emotional or sonic intent when they are missing.
Then sketch each section's role and what changes or is intentionally preserved.

## Content and singability

- Prefer short, natural phrases that a voice can deliver without strain.
- Anchor verses in a concrete image: who, where, when when the writing needs
  it. Let the chorus carry the feeling or thesis when there is one.
- Every line follows from the previous one. Do not force a word to complete a
  verse or a rhyme.
- Prefer showing to telling. Avoid philosophical language, complicated
  metaphors and filler phrases; give clichés a specific detail from this song.
- Put the stressed syllable on a strong beat. Do not require the same syllable
  count in every line; lines sung to the same melody should stay close in
  length. Leave room to breathe or sustain at the end of a line.
- Avoid long words, heavy consonant clusters and tangled sequences of "s",
  "r" or similar sounds.
- Rhyme only when it comes naturally, with simple words. Internal and slant
  rhyme usually sound more natural than strict AABB. The rhyme type should fit
  the meaning: resolved or ambiguous.
- Keep the addressee consistent when the writing uses direct address. The
  speaker knows only what they can see or feel; use observable behavior rather
  than explaining another person's mind.
- Do not mention instruments, production terms, sections, genres, key or BPM
  in the sung text unless they are the subject of the song.
- Short vocal interjections are optional, judged case by case, and belong in
  the lyric field; a clean song is always valid.

## Title

A title is short, one to five words in the language of the lyrics, tied to the
hook or the central idea, with no brackets. It is identification, not a field
of its own inside the song text.

## Pre-delivery check

Read each line aloud and sing it slowly. Replace a word when the tongue
trips, the accent falls strangely, the word needs explanation, the line does
not connect to the previous one, or the rhyme is forced. Then check the number
of lines against the structure, the position of section marks, the title
against the hook, and the lyrics against the style's tempo and voice. One pass
is enough; report a remaining discrepancy instead of rewriting repeatedly.

## Library consultation

Optional and targeted. Use `references/library/rhythm/` for prosody and
`references/library/harmony/` for topline or harmonic color when they change a
decision. Use `references/library/lyrics/` when the brief calls for subtext,
layered meaning or more distinctive word choice. Never load more than the
pointed section, and never let a reference replace the rules above.

## Coherence and output

Lyrics respect Style (genre, voice, tempo) and the form planned with
Arrangement. Section marks come from `references/instructions/performance.md`
and sit above their blocks. Presentation in the final delivery format follows
`references/instructions/orchestration.md`, "Final delivery format (mandatory)".

For a provided reference, follow "Musical references" in orchestration.md:
translate into generic descriptors, keep the section order without copying
words or melody, and do not invent anything about an inaccessible source.
