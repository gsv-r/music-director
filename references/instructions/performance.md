# Musical performance direction

The result is textual: audible indications written into the lyric sections.
It never produces audio. See `references/instructions/best-practices.md` for
the criteria and `references/instructions/orchestration.md` for universal rules.

## Section markers

Name a section when it needs execution guidance: `[Intro]`, `[Verse]`,
`[Pre-Chorus]`, `[Chorus]`, `[Bridge]`, `[Outro]`, `[Fade Out]`, or an
instrumental-only label. Add up to three comma-separated audible indications
after a hyphen: `[Section - indication, indication]`. Put the marker alone on
its line, above the block it describes; do not split it into two consecutive
markers or drop one in the middle of a verse. Keep sung text and indications
visually separate. Four to eight enriched indications across a full song is a
heuristic, not a target.

## What makes an indication useful

- Describe something that can be heard: intensity, voice, beat, instrument
  entry, attack, articulation, register, timing or sustain.
- Prefer a named behavior ("straight tone", "short breath", "soft attack",
  "spoken word") to a generic intensity adjective.
- Translate the intention into one or two audible parameters and keep the
  three-indication limit. Long instructions do not help.
- Describe this passage. Do not assign a default intensity, density or final
  surge to a section type, and do not repeat what is already in the Style.
- A crescendo made by playing harder is an execution choice; a crescendo made
  by adding layers is an arrangement decision. Do not use a marker to invent a
  layer entry that was not planned.
- Markers are guidance, not guarantees. If a direction is not followed,
  reformulate it more clearly rather than adding more markers.

## Vocabulary

- Registers: chest voice, head voice, falsetto, mixed voice.
- Delivery: whispered, breathy singing, spoken word, conversational, rap,
  belting, deadpan, monotone.
- Technique: vibrato, straight tone, legato, staccato, twang, breathy singing,
  grit or rasp, cry or sob, runs, melisma.
- Dynamics and timing: crescendo or diminuendo, dynamic contrast, accent,
  rubato, push or pull, breath points, behind or ahead of the beat.
- Vocal arrangement: harmonies, doubles, choir, humming, backing vocals,
  ad-libs.
- Local execution: no drums, solo bass, breakdown, dry stop, stripped ending.

Use only the terms that are heard and fit the genre, the emotion and the
arrangement. A polished, precise performance remains valid when intended.

## Vocal enrichment

Ad-libs, interjections and vocalizes can enrich a piece when the genre has
space for them, they support the emotion and groove, and they respect the
prosody. Judge each case: dense storytelling, minimalist writing, a saturated
arrangement or a song that works better clean are reasons to leave them out.
One to three per song is usually enough. When in doubt, keep it clean.

## Development across the song

Plan internally what changes from one appearance of a section to the next:
register, intensity, phrasing, space or silence. Exact repetition is valid.
Write any changed word or line in the lyrics; a marker cannot create it.
Derive the final section's indication from what actually changes or from
intentional stability.

## Library consultation

Consult `references/library/index.md` when a term or audible effect needs
grounding; read only the pointed section. The `references/library/performance/`
entries are the source for execution vocabulary. Targeted use of
`references/library/rhythm/` for timing and `references/library/production/`
for vocal processing is optional. Consulting is not applying; an entry becomes
an audible indication only when it fits this composition.

## Coherence and output

Markers must match the Style's voice, tempo and production, the form from
Arrangement and the emotion of the lyrics. Do not duplicate global sound or
form here. Presentation in the final delivery format follows
`references/instructions/orchestration.md`, "Final delivery format (mandatory)".
